first-picnic|Sights|Maplewold|My First Picnic|Bramble Mossfoot says a picnic is just a walk with snacks at the end, and she is right.\n\nYou sniffed, picked, trimmed, peeked, dressed a scarecrow and blew a hundred wishes before you sat down on Picnic Rise. Every Sight works the same way: stand next to it, press its button, and it grows back in about five and a half game days.\n\n[b]Bramble's tip:[/b] the honey always tastes better on the rise.
clover-smiled|Sights|Maplewold|Clover Smiled|It was very brief. Some say it was the wind. But on the day the pancake stack wobbled in Pancake Lane, Clover Puddlejump of the Maplewold Market smiled, and the Sugar Shack has the spoon to prove it.\n\nMaple sap only runs in spring, so the Tap Row is a spring Sight.
which-nettle|Sights|Maplewold|Which Nettle?|There are two Nettle Cobblefields in the Maplewold: a chatty cricket herbalist in Cloverholt and an eager wood-mouse keeper at the Mossgate shrine. For years their post went to the wrong one.\n\nNow the signpost at the Honeybrook crossroads has a fifth arrow: [b]Nettles: BOTH WAYS[/b]. The cricket gets the candle orders. The mouse gets the nettle tea. Mostly.
lantern-home|Sights|Maplewold|The Lantern That Came Home|Keeper Marram's little lantern wandered off down the shore one foggy evening and was found on the nose of a seal pup, who would like it known that it was only borrowing it.\n\nIt hangs on the Lantern Rocks again now, and if you hum at dusk, it hums back.
letter-from-the-south|Sights|Maplewold|A Letter from the South|The Great White Owl read your letter slowly, twice, holding it very gently in one enormous claw. It was from her cousin in the south, who says the weather is warm and the mice are cheeky.\n\nShe is the boss of her Den, but she is kind, and kindness has a soft spot. Walk in gently.
great-white-owl|Sights|Maplewold|The Great White Owl|Two big kind eyes in the dark of the Den, blinking slowly. Blink slowly back and she knows you mean no harm.\n\nThe Den of the Great White Owl is a lair at the end of the Cloverholt road. A lair is won by besting its boss or by befriending it.
woodland-census|Sights|Maplewold|Wren's Woodland Census|One sleepy owl, one polite spider, one sunbathing rabbit, one toad on the top step, one very slow woodpecker and one squirrel who was absolutely not watching.\n\nWren Thimblewick wrote them all into Tansy's old field guide, and found Tansy's pin flag, [b]Good apples[/b], still stuck in a ring of the Thousand Rings Stump. Wren went a bit misty.
lunch-signpost|Sights|Maplewold|The Lunch Signpost|Four arrows at the Honeybrook crossroads, and every one of them points at lunch.\n\nVillagers move about: on about one game day in four a villager is away visiting family, and you may meet them on the road instead. Read the sign to hear who is away from your start town today.
mr-pleased|Sights|Maplewold|Mr. Pleased, a Year in Hats|The scarecrow on the Poppyburrow road has been dressed by you four times now, and he has never looked more pleased with himself. A daisy, a straw boater, a carrot nose and, once, everybody's spare scarf.\n\nThe crows say he is unbearable. They visit every day.
sir-trickle|Sights|Maplewold|Sir Trickle|After ten good bows, the stone bridge on the Cloverholt road finally tells you its name: [b]Sir Trickle[/b], of the Brook.\n\nIt has kept the letter cubby for two hundred years and has never once read the post. It would like that written down. Now it is.
sparrow-and-sons|Sights|Maplewold|Sparrow & Sons, Hauliers|The sparrow family in the hay wagon's driving seat run a small haulage business. They have never moved the wagon one inch, but they have very strong opinions about the road.\n\nFeed them five times and they budge up so you can sit in the driving seat. Do not touch the reins. The reins are decorative.
wheel-bows|Sights|Maplewold|The Wheel That Bowed|The pond mill's wheel turns for the fun of it. After your fifth winning leaf boat it stopped, for exactly one turn, and bowed.\n\nNobody in Poppyburrow believes you. The ducks saw it.
grotto-waltz|Sights|Maplewold|The Grotto Waltz|On rainy days the Drip Grotto stops keeping time and plays a whole tune: plip, plop, plip-plip, plop. Lyra Fiddlewing the minstrel wrote it down for you.\n\nIt is a waltz, more or less. Mostly less.
two-inches|Sights|Maplewold|Two Inches|Inside the toadstool ring you always feel exactly one inch taller. At dusk, in the fog, you felt two.\n\nYou measured yourself at the Oak Arch afterwards. One inch. The ring says it does not do refunds.
the-aurora|Sights|Maplewold|The Aurora|On clear tundra nights the sky over the north of the Maplewold does slow green and pink dances. Gnomes call it the aurora; the lemmings call it the ceiling.\n\nAt Aurora Ledge you can see it through the clear ice. Gaze at night for a little Luck.
tired-sofa|Sights|Maplewold|The Very Tired Sofa, a Biography|Born a tree. Became a log. Fell in the sea, which it did not enjoy. Washed up on the Lighthouse shore and was taken, in turn, for a dragon, a chair and a sofa.\n\nIt has decided it is a sofa. It is very tired. Please sit on it.
hired|Sights|Maplewold|Hired!|The Beaver Foreman inspected your patch on the dam, slapped his tail twice, and said the word every gnome wants to hear: [b]Hired![/b]\n\nYou do not get paid. Everything is a lumber order. But you are on the crew list now, in pencil.
toast-aurora|Sights|Maplewold|Toast under the Aurora|At Aurora Camp there is always a banked fire, a kettle and somebody with a marshmallow. At night the green lights dance overhead while you toast your toes.\n\nNobody has ever toasted a marshmallow perfectly here. Everyone agrees that is not the point.
owl-thanks|Sights|Maplewold|A Compliment on the Mat|You wiped your feet on the Owl's moss doormat three times, and she noticed every time.\n\nThis morning there was a small, tidy owl pellet on the mat and a note in very large handwriting: [b]Thank you for your manners.[/b]
record-stone|Sights|Maplewold|The Skipping Record Stone|Fennel Crumblewick of Pebblestead keeps the pebble-skipping record on a flat stone by the pebble bar. The record is seven skips.\n\nYour name is on it now, in Fennel's neat chalk, with a small star next to it.
flag-lessons|Sights|Maplewold|Signal Flags|Wick Kettleby of Pebblestead is a signaller and is teaching the rock-pool crab his flags. One flag up: hello. Two flags: lunch. Both flags waved wildly: the tide is coming in, run.\n\nThe crab learned faster than you. It has more arms.
tide-note|Sights|Maplewold|The Causeway Tide Note|Burdock Oakenpot the cartographer has timed the tide on the Lighthouse causeway with his pocket watch for nine years.\n\nHis note for you: the mussels are best at dawn and dusk, the samphire any time, and nobody has fallen in. Nobody who will admit it.
fennicks-wish|Sights|Maplewold|Fennick's Old Wish|Years ago, lying on Sky Hill in the snow, Fennick Hollowell wished for a shop that sold only very small things. He still has not opened it.\n\nHe says the wish is not used up. It is just resting.
bee-sign|Sights|Maplewold|The Bees' Sign|The beehive on the Honeybrook road had a sign: NO VISTERS. Then NO VISSITORS. Then NO VIZZITORS.\n\nMabel Pennywhistle, who calls the bees her cousins, fixed it to NO VISITORS. The bees said thank you and went straight on letting visitors in.
thousand-rings|Sights|Maplewold|The Thousand Rings Stump|The great stump on the Cloverholt forest road has more rings than anyone has ever counted the same way twice. Somewhere near the middle is Tansy's old pin flag that says [b]Good apples[/b].\n\nTrace a ring with your finger and it tells you a little story about whoever was alive when it grew.
willas-tale|Sights|Maplewold|The Five-Part Echo|Willa Gillyflower of Shellhollow collects echoes in jars. Five echoes make a story: a frog, a boat, a very large hat, a storm, and the frog again, now wearing the hat.\n\nIt is the best story in Shellhollow. Willa says it is also true.
hundred-wishes|Sights|Maplewold|The Dandelion Clocks|Every dandelion clock on the Cloverholt road holds a hundred wishes, and you have blown ten of them now. That is a thousand wishes.\n\nThe dandelions grow back in about five and a half game days. The wishes take longer.
