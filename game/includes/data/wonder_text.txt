Thousand Rings Stump|A hollow stump as wide as a village green. Each ring in the wood is a year, and some of them seem to be whispering.|You find a ring that spells out a hidden seed store, and a pouch tucked inside it.|The rings blur together, and you can’t find the page you want. The wood forgives you.
Lighthouse of the Lost Lantern|The old lighthouse has gone dark. Its keeper, a retired crab, looks up hopefully, holding a wick.|The lamp catches, and the keeper whoops. The beam sweeps the shore, and the sea gives something back.|The wick fizzles in the wind. The keeper pats your shoulder: “Next tide, perhaps.”
The Stepped Seed Temple|Terraces climb a hillside in steps, each carved with a riddle that has been waiting centuries for an answer.|The last step clicks, and a small door in the wall swings open on the old seed store.|The riddle stays a riddle. The steps are warm in the sun, though, and the view is something.
Cenote of Echoes|A round pool drops into the earth, deep and green. Whatever you say comes back, wiser and slightly late.|Your voice returns with a glint of something at the bottom. You bring it up, dripping and delighted.|The echo returns only a polite cough. The water is cold, and the bottom keeps its treasures.
Sunken Gnome Galleon|A tiny ship lies on its side in the reef shallows, its hold full of barnacled chests and a very surprised octopus.|You prise one chest open. The octopus nods grudgingly, and something shiny is yours.|The chests are stuck fast, and the octopus is getting grumpy. Perhaps tomorrow.
The Singing Waterfall|Water tumbles down a green cliff in a long, clear note. Flowers grow where the spray lands, and not a single one is ordinary.|You find a ledge behind the curtain, bright with rare flowers, and a cutting comes away in your hand.|The spray soaks you and the cliff stays shy. The waterfall hums a sympathetic note.
The Sky City Ruins|Old stone walls climb above the clouds. Doorways open on nothing, and stairs lead to the sky.|One staircase leads somewhere after all. Behind a loose stone sits a small, careful hoard.|The stairs go up and up and end in air. You come down with a good view and nothing else.
Pink River Dolphin Pools|Gentle pink dolphins roll in shallow pools. Seeds drift in the current, and the dolphins seem to know where.|A dolphin nudges a bundle of seeds toward you and squeaks. Best of friends, you two.|The dolphins are busy with a game of their own. They flick water at you, kindly.
The Mirror Salt Flat|After rain, the flat is a perfect mirror. Sky above, sky below, and you walking in the middle of both.|In the reflection, a glint on the ground that isn’t in the sky. You follow it and find it.|It’s all sky and no ground, and you stumble around happily. The flat gives nothing today.
The Standing Stones|Mossy stones in a ring, humming softly. At dusk they hum louder, as if they’d like to be asked something.|You ask the right question, and the nearest stone gives up a seed, dropped from its hollow top.|The stones hum, but they won’t be drawn. You lean on one, and it feels like a pat on the back.
Fairy-ring Clearing|A perfect ring of mushrooms, and the mycelium underneath is humming. Something is going on beneath your boots.|The ring brightens, and a small door opens at its edge. A tiny hand passes you a gift.|The ring sulks and the mushrooms close up. You leave, politely, backing out.
Goat Path Vineyards|Terraced vines on a steep hillside, and a harvest in full swing. The goats are supervising.|The head picker presses a bundle of rare cuttings into your hands. “For the help.”|You tangle yourself in the vines. The goats laugh in the way goats do.
The Labyrinth Isle|A hedge maze on a green island, with a stone gnome at its heart who has been asking the same riddle for centuries.|You answer it, and the gnome yawns with relief. At its feet, a small surprise.|The hedges rearrange themselves while you look away. You emerge at the start, a little dizzy.
The Amber Woods|Amber glows in the trees, and some of it holds ancient seeds, caught in the sap. They’re very fragile.|You ease a piece free without a crack, and the seed inside is whole and warm to the touch.|The amber snaps in your fingers. A little sorry, a little sticky, you go home with the story.
Stork Chimney Village|Storks nest on every chimney, and every beak holds something: a letter, a seed, a ribbon, a very small shoe.|A stork drops a parcel at your feet, as if it had been waiting for you all this time.|The storks are busy delivering and don’t see you. Their shadows pass overhead, graceful.
The Hanging Garden Ruins|Terraces of old stone, and crops still growing in them after a thousand years because nobody told them to stop.|You find a terrace gone wild, and the plants there press their finest on you.|The terraces are steep, the heat is high, and the plants seem to be asleep. Tomorrow.
Cedar Sanctuary|Ancient cedars, so quiet that you hear your own thoughts for the first time all week.|You sit and breathe, and something that was tangled in your head comes loose. A seed falls into your lap.|Your thoughts will not stay still. The cedars don’t mind. They’ve outlasted worse.
The Oasis That Moves|The oasis turns up in a different village each day. Today it’s here, cool and green and slightly smug about it.|You spot it before the others do, and the date palms drop a few treasures in your direction.|It shimmers, you reach, it moves. Today the oasis is quicker than you.
Mud Mosque Library|A tall mud-brick library, cool inside, its shelves full of old farming scrolls. The guide is a tortoise with spectacles.|The tortoise finds the very scroll you need and points to a note in the margin that unlocks something.|The scrolls are in an old script, and the tortoise has gone for a nap. You tiptoe out.
Gorilla Glade|Quiet giants share a glade of forest fruit. They watch you politely, waiting for your manners.|You greet them properly, and the largest hands you a fruit, as one would to a visiting aunt.|You say the wrong thing, or the right thing in the wrong order. They look away, offended.
Coffee Ceremony Hut|A little hut and three cups: one to wake, one to talk, one to bless. The host pours with great ceremony.|The third cup comes with a blessing, and a little extra energy too. You leave humming.|You spill the second cup, or perhaps the host spills it for you. Either way, there’s a lesson.
The Great Migration|Herds in their thousands thunder past, and hidden in the dust are little treasures kicked up by the hooves.|You pick a lucky spot and snatch a find from the dust as the herd rolls by.|The dust swallows you, and by the time you can see again, they’re gone.
Flamingo Shallows|A pink lake, pink birds, and pink-feathered crafters trading their wares from tiny stalls.|You strike a fair bargain, and a crafter slips something extra into your bag for the company.|They are all lovely, and none of them wants what you’ve brought. You wade home with pink knees.
The Silk Road Caravanserai|A trade stop with a courtyard and a fountain, and travelling gnomes from a dozen lands, all swapping seeds.|You swap well, and a trader with a twinkle adds a rarity “for the road”.|Nobody wants your seeds today. You fill your afternoon with gossip instead, which is also a treasure.
Tea Garden Slopes|Rows of tea on a mist-wrapped slope. The pickers move like a slow river, and the air smells like a warm cup.|You pick the best two leaves and a bud, just as shown. The head picker nods and slips you a treasure.|Your leaves are the wrong leaves. The head picker is very kind about it.
Prayer-flag Pass|Colourful flags flutter along a high pass, and each one carries a wish. The wind reads them all out loud.|You tie your flag, and the wind catches it with a snap. Somewhere, a wish is granted, and something lands at your feet.|Your knot slips, and your flag flies off over the ridge. It will be read somewhere else.
The Thousand Gates|Red gates march up the hillside, thousands of them, and each one asks a little thing of you as you walk through.|You pass them all, one by one, and at the last gate there’s a bowl and something shining in it.|The gates are tall and your legs are short. You stop at a few hundred and sit with the view.
Pearl Diver Cove|A calm cove and a diver’s raft. Beneath it, the oysters wait, and they’re very particular.|You hold your breath just long enough and surface with a pearl, a rare thing, and a very proud grin.|You surface empty-handed and out of breath. The oysters keep their pearls for another day.
Panda Bamboo Grove|A pandas’ grove, all rustle and munch. One panda has a splinter, and one very serious expression.|You help ease out the splinter, and the panda shares an armful of bamboo shoots. It is a grand honour.|The panda was fine all along and looks at you, bemused. It goes back to chewing.
Lantern Festival River|Paper lanterns drift down a dark river, each one a wish, and you’re handed one to float.|Your lantern sails clear to the bend, and the glow reflects something good back at you.|Your lantern spins, tips, and sinks. A kindly boatman fishes it out and tells you it still counts.
Volcano Spice Gardens|Terraces of spice on the slopes of a sleepy volcano. The air tingles on your tongue, and the soil is hot to touch.|The gardeners trust you with a precious cutting, wrapped in leaf and still warm.|The heat gets to you before the spices do. You fan yourself with your hat and retreat.
Root-wrapped Temple|A temple held in the arms of tree roots, a stone face looking out between the knuckles of the wood.|You read the old script between the roots, and a hidden panel swings open.|The roots have kept the script covered. You trace it with a finger and learn very little.
Glowworm Grotto|A cavern ceiling studded with tiny living stars, and the silence between them as big as the cave.|You lie still, and the glowworms spell out a pattern. Below it, a niche holds a find.|The stars blink out when you cough. By the time they’re back, you’ve lost your place.
Starpath Canoe|A great canoe waits on the sand, its navigators reading the stars. “Sit,” they say, “and learn the sea’s way.”|You read the stars as they taught you, and they let you steer for a bit. A gift follows.|The stars wheel the wrong way for you. The navigators smile: “It takes a lifetime.”
Stone Head Watchers|Giant carved heads sit in a row on the headland, each one old, patient, and ready to answer one question.|You ask the right thing, and the head’s eyes close in a slow, satisfied blink. Something rolls out from its lip.|Your question was a muddle. The head stays silent, and you realise it was a good one anyway.
