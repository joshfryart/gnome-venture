Trail tiles in the Hearth Pop v3 style

Proposal and mockup. The scenes below are the real Maplewold maps (11x7 sections) drawn with proposed tile art, with the real v3 gnome-player sprite (64x80, 3/4 top-down) standing in them. Red-outlined tiles are placeholders: S start, 9 way on, E fight, F find. Art is generated by gen.py as a stand-in; final tiles would be drawn in Aseprite like the rest of v3.

The idea in one picture

Square grid, 3/4 oblique view. The grid stays square, so the 44 px tap buttons, tilemap.js and the 9x9 camera do not change. What changes is that things that stand up are taller than their tile (trees, rocks, hedges, pines), exactly like the player sprite is taller than its tile. They are drawn over the tile above them and depth-sorted by their feet row, so the gnome walks behind a tree that is south of them and in front of one that is north of them.

The kit: 4 lands, 6 ground tiles + 3 raised objects each

Left to right per row: 4 floor variants (.), 2 rough variants (,), then raised walls (#). Coast and tundra also get a flat sea / ice tile for the water edge and the frozen pond.

Scale against the player

Tile 64x64, gnome cell 64x80 with the figure about 48 px tall (0.75 tile), feet baseline y=70. In a scene the gnome's feet sit 46 px into their tile, so the body stays inside the tile and only the hat pokes into the tile above. A tree is about 1.4 tiles tall and the crown reaches at most ~13 px into the tile above, so a gnome standing north of a tree is never hidden by it.

Rules (the v3 guide, applied to tiles)

TopicRule
SizeGround tile 64x64 (same pixel density as the 64x80 sprite cell; the game's 42-unit tile is 64 x 0.656, a CSS scale, or TM_UNIT becomes 64). Raised object canvas 64x96, bottom-aligned, drawn 12 px below the tile top-of-row line so the base sits on its tile.
View3/4 top-down, square grid. You see a tree's crown and trunk, a rock's top plane and front face, a hedge's top and front. No diamond projection.
GroundOpaque, seamless (details wrap), full-bleed, no outline, no tile-wide shade. Two-tone mottling, flecks, tufts: no dither, no gradient. 4 floor + 2 rough variants per land, swap with no seam.
Raised objects1 px warm ink #2b1c26 outside the whole object only. Lit dome per pixel from a normal field snapped to the 4-tone ramp (hi base shade deep), light from the upper left, one shade side lower-right. A dark ambient row where crown meets trunk or object meets ground. Leaf, grain or crack speckle for material.
Flat thingsWater, ice, mud and paths are ground: no outline. Water has 2-hi ripple dashes with a shade dash under.
DepthSort by feet row (y + 0.5); sprites and raised objects share one sort. Contact shadow: a hard-pixel wedge one tone darker on the ground to the lower right of each raised object. Ground under walls is one tone darker than the walkable floor so the trail edge reads before any outline.
ColourHearth Pop ramps (moss, leaf, pine, wood, stone, sea, sand, snow). Saturated bases, cool-violet shadows, warm-yellow lights. No pure black or white. Floors stay lighter than walls; rough floor is a clear step away from floor but never dark enough to look like a wall.
QuirkOne charming tell per raised object, not repeated: berries on a tree, blossom on a bush, moss on a rock, a snowy hem on a pine.
Game marksS 9 E F stay as the game's own markers on top (red-outlined here). The player token is the real 2.5D sprite; E and F can be small sprites later (a rustle in the grass, a sparkle) drawn on the same sorting rule.

Open points for you

  • Tile size: 64 px native with a CSS scale to 44+ px on phones keeps one pixel density with the sprite. Non-integer scale can shimmer; the 9x9 camera on a 7x7 phone window would be about 63 px per tile, nearly 1:1.
  • Edge joins: this mockup uses plain per-tile ground. If you want soft coast and pond edges, the trek-pixel 47-tile auto-join recipe applies unchanged to water, ice and rough.
  • Animation: optional 2-frame water and a 2-frame tree sway, CSS only, off in data-saver.
  • Final art: this is generated; the two-pass Aseprite/Lua path in the guide (Pass A form, Pass B polish) would produce the shipping tiles, 4 lands first.
