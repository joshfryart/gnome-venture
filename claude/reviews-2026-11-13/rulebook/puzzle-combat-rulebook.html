1 What you are doing

Puzzle combat is a small board game inside Gnome Venture. You meet a creature. You both put jigsaw pieces on a 4 by 4 board. Pieces that link fire together as a chain. This book describes the rules as they are in a new fight today (version 2.6.986, puzzle combat v1, still switched on for admins only).

1.1 The goal

Fill the creature's Yield bar before your health reaches 0. That is the whole game. played

1.2 The Yield bar

  • The creature has one bar. It starts empty, for example Yield 0/43.
  • Your Hurt pieces fill it. Your Charm pieces fill the same bar. played
  • When it is full the creature yields and you win.
  • The label counts both parts: "Yield 23/40: hurt 15, charm 8". code

How much a piece adds is not always its number:

1.3 Your health

  • Your health is the health of your expedition. You bring it into the fight and you take what is left out of it. code
  • The creature's Hurt pieces lower it. So do Bramble, Thornburst and poison.
  • Guard blocks the creature's Hurt pieces. Guard does not block Bramble, Thornburst or poison. played
  • At 0 you are knocked out and the fight is lost.

1.4 One round

Figure 1. A round. You act once, then you watch.
  • You do one action: place one piece, or pass. Flipping a piece is free and is not an action.
  • Then the creature plays. Then the environment places one piece. Then old pieces leave and you draw a new hand.
  • Your whole hand is thrown away and redrawn every round. You cannot save a piece for later. played

2 The pieces

2.1 What is on a piece

Every piece has a name, a type, a number, four edges and sometimes a tag.

2.3 Edges, fitting and linking

  • Each edge is a knob (solid, sticks out) or a hole (hollow, cut in).
  • Each knob and hole belongs to a family: body (round), mind (keyhole) or craft (triangle).
  • Two edges fit when one is a knob and the other a hole of the same family.
  • You may place a piece on an empty cell only if it touches at least one piece and every edge that touches a piece fits. played
  • A joint that fits is a link. Chains run along links.
Figure 3. Fitting. There is no "almost": a touching edge fits or the cell is closed to that piece.

Two special edges exist:

  • Wild: fits anything. The Blank has four wild edges, but no chain runs through the Blank. A Plant's own Bramble also has wild edges, and it does link. code
  • Flat: fits anything and never links. Today only Offer (flat right edge) has one. Mud, which is out of the game for now, is flat all round. code

How your pieces are cut

You do not need to learn this by heart, the lit cells show where a piece goes. But the pattern helps you plan:

The top and bottom family follows where the piece comes from: tool and craft pieces craft, pal pieces body, crop pieces mind, all others the family of their type (PFB_SRC_FAM, pfb_author_edges). Checked against every hand I was dealt. played

What follows from it: a Hurt or a Guard can sit to the right of any piece with a body knob on its right. Pieces of the same column family stack above and below each other, knob-on-top under hole-on-top.

2.4 Flipping

Figure 4. Flip.
  • Flip is free and unlimited. Flip the piece, look at the new lit cells, flip it back if you like. played
  • A flipped piece stays flipped on the board. It is the right way up again when it comes back into your hand.
  • Flip matters when the only free cell next to your target is on the wrong side. In my Carrion Crow fight the flipped Pierce reached a cell worth 18. Unflipped, that cell was closed. played

The order rule that decides most turns

The piece you place fires first. So to get Ambush you place the Trick next to a Hurt that is already on the board. To get Riposte you place the Guard next to a Hurt that is already there. Placing the Hurt next to the Trick gives no combo at all.

The creature gets the same combos from its own pieces. Its aim names them ("combo Double Swing").

2.8 Where the numbers come from

  • A piece's number is its base value, plus your stat for that type, plus a third of your gnome level, plus a rank bonus (Uncommon +2, Rare +4, Masterwork +5). code
  • Stats by type: Hurt uses Grit, Guard Nimble, Heal Hardiness, Charm and Soothe Charm, Trick and Item Lore. code
  • A tool piece wears its tool: 2 durability each time you play it. A chip in the fight shows the wear. code

3 Setting up a fight

Everything in this chapter is read from the page code (pfdeck.php, pfdeck_view.php). I did not open the live pages. not verified

3.1 The Puzzle box and your deck

  • Your deck is built in the Puzzle box. It has three tabs: Kit, Belt, Pals.
  • The first time, you pack a starter: 4 fixed pieces (Swing, Dodge, Rest, Charm) plus 6 of 12 offered pieces. One tap picks a ready kit instead:
    • Bold (the default): your calling piece, Jab, a second Swing, Pierce, Snack, Oat Cake.
    • Steady: calling piece, Jab, second Swing, Pierce, Brace, your home region's piece.
    • Kind: calling piece, Jab, second Swing, Pierce, Hum, Soothe.
  • Later you switch on more pieces you own: knacks (by level), crop pieces, craft pieces, region pieces.
  • Limits: at least 8 kit pieces, at least 3 Hurt pieces in a custom starter, at most 3 Rare and 1 Masterwork. There is no maximum size.
  • The deck bar shows "Hurt pieces: N of M". Under 3 in 10 it warns: "Few Hurt pieces: fights will be long".

3.2 The belt

  • Each tool on your fight belt adds its one piece to the deck (axe: Chop; tilling tool: Hoe Hook; staff: Sweep; lantern: Glare; and so on).
  • Belt slots: 2 for every gnome, 3 with the Stitched Fight Belt (level 8), 4 with the Masterwork Fight Belt (level 18).
  • A tool has 20 durability, plus 4 per rank. Each play of its piece costs 2. A broken tool gives no piece. Fix it with a Tool Repair Kit, or Remove it from the belt.

3.3 Pals

Pals in your party lend one piece each (for example Bark Badger, a Guard; Samara Spin, a Hurt). Pal pieces have body tops and bottoms.

3.4 The pre-fight screen

  • When a creature stands in front of you, you see its creature card, your deck and your health, and the button Let's fight.
  • With Auto deck on, the deck is locked and you only press the button. With it off, tap a deck piece to take it out or a bench piece to add it.
  • "Nothing changes once the fight starts." The deck is locked during the fight.

3.5 Reading the creature card

What to check on the card before you press Let's fight

  • The class rule. It changes how you answer the aim.
  • Tags on its pieces. Quick means two pieces in one turn. Pierce goes through your guard. Poison keeps costing health. Evade makes your next Hurt miss. Thorns punish hitting its guard.
  • Temper. Against a gentle creature Charm is the stronger type. Against a wary one it is the weaker.
  • Your Hurt count. In my runs almost every win above small size was a hurt win.

The card prints "stays N rounds" and "plays x2" per piece. "Plays x2" is right. The rounds number is not used in a new fight (10.3).

4 The opening

Figure 5. The start of my first fight. Cells are named x,y from the top left: the Blank is on 2,2.

4.1 The board and the Blank

  • The board is 4 by 4 for every creature. played
  • One Blank piece starts on a random one of the four inner cells. played
  • The Blank fits every edge. It is inert: it never fires and a chain never passes through it. It leaves at the end of round 1. played

4.2 The start pieces

  1. You get your first hand of 5.
  2. You pick one hand piece and one side of the Blank (left, right, above, below).
  3. The creature has already picked: its gentlest piece that is not a Hurt, and a random side. If you chose the same side, yours stays and its piece moves to the next free side code. A boss puts out two pieces. played
  4. Nothing fires. Turn 1 begins with your other 4 pieces still in hand.

4.3 Choosing your start piece

Guideline: start with a Hurt, and check its partner

  • Put a Hurt out. On turn 1 you can then place a Trick or a Guard next to it and fire it with a combo.
  • Before you confirm, find the partner in your hand and the side it must go on. Leave that cell free: do not put the Hurt where the partner's cell is off the board or is the Blank.
  • The start piece is not wasted if it is used up later. It usually fires two or three times.

Example with numbers

Earwig, seed 11. Hand: Chop (Hurt 5), Slip Past (Trick 6), Swing, Dodge, Frost Bark. I put Chop left of the Blank. Slip Past can then go above, below or left of Chop. Turn 1: Slip Past above Chop, Ambush, 14 of 42.8 on the bar. played

Wild Boar, seed 3. Hand: Hoe Hook (Hurt 15), Swing 12, Pierce 11, Oat Cake, Garden Greens. Hoe Hook below the Blank. Turn 1: Swing to its right also touched the creature's start piece: Swing 12, then Hoe Hook with Double Swing 17, in all 29 of 171.8. played

4.4 What the creature does in the opening

  • Its start piece is harmless when placed. But it stays, and it fires for the creature if a chain reaches it.
  • Its first aim is shown as soon as both start pieces are down. Read it before your first move.
  • During the start pick an aim mark is already on the board. It is worked out before the start pieces exist and is replaced once they are down. Ignore it. played

5 Your turn, step by step

A turn is: look, pick, (flip), place, read the plan, End turn. Three or four taps. The thinking is in "look" and "read the plan".

5.2 Step 2: pick a piece

  • Tap a hand piece. The cells where it may go light up. Each lit cell carries numbers: what the chain adds to the bar, and what it costs you.
  • Tap Flip and look again. Every piece has two shapes.
  • Check every piece. Hands are small, and the best cell is often on a piece you did not expect. In my Great Hawk fight the best move of the whole fight was a Trick with the number 20 that read "+77".

Read the health cost on the cell

A lit cell can cost you health. Two things cause it: your chain reaches a creature Hurt (it fires for the creature, at you), or your chain reaches a Thornburst (you lose a tenth of your health). In my 114 "biggest number" fights the player lost 13% of its health this way; the careful player 5%.

5.3 Step 3: place it and read the plan

  • Tap a lit cell. The piece is down but the turn is not over.
  • The plan line lists your chain in firing order and the result ("Yield 12 to 23 of 40 (hurt +11)").
  • The aim chip updates: it now says what the creature's move would do after your placement. It may say the aim is broken.
  • Not happy? Pick again. Nothing counts until End turn.

5.4 How a chain fires

  1. The piece you placed fires first.
  2. Then every piece linked to it fires, the newest first. From each of those the chain goes on through its own links before it comes back. A piece fires once per chain.
  3. Each piece fires for its owner: yours for you, the creature's for the creature.
  4. An environment piece fires for the side that started the chain.
  5. If the fight ends in the middle, the rest of the chain does not fire.

Figure 7. The Earwig’s answer in the same round. I planned this: see "Bait" below.

What it means when a creature piece is in your chain

  • Its Hurt hits you. Its Guard gives the creature guard that soaks your later Hurt. Its Heal empties the bar. Its tag works for it (Evade, Thorns, Poison, Trip).
  • But it also spends one of that piece's two firings. A creature piece that has fired twice falls off the board.
  • So firing a harmless creature piece (a Trick with no tag, a small Guard when you play Charm or Pierce) is a cheap way to remove it.

Bait: what it means when your piece is in the creature's chain

  • Your piece fires for you, with your combos, in the creature's turn. played
  • The aim is fixed before you move. If you put a piece next to the aim cell with an edge that fits, the creature's piece will link to it and fire it.
  • In the Earwig fight the creature's own chains gave me 14 + 4 + 4 = 22 of the 42.8 I needed.
  • The aim chip shows it after you place: "fires your Chop: 14 to it".
  • Bait fails when the creature has Evade up or gets it earlier in that chain: your Hurt misses.

5.5 The chain of 3 rule (use-up)

  • A chain that fires 3 or more pieces uses up every piece that fired. They leave the board at once. played
  • It does not matter who started the chain or who owns the pieces. Environment pieces count and are used up too.
  • A chain of 1 or 2 uses nothing up. The pieces stay and can fire again next turn.
  • Used-up pieces of yours go to your discard pile. You will draw them again later.

When is a chain of 3 worth it?

Usually yes. The board empties all the time anyway: in my careful runs I had no piece of my own on the board at the start of 51% of my turns. A piece you "save" is often used up by the creature's chain, or it expires. Take the 3-chain when one of these is true:

  • It adds clearly more to the bar than the best chain of 2. In the Crow fight, turn 2: 18 against 7.
  • Your pieces in it are in their last round ("leaves this round").
  • It uses up a creature piece you want gone (a Hurt next to the aim, an Evade piece).
  • It ends the fight.

Keep the chain at 2 when it fires a Bramble, Sun or Rain that you can fire again next turn and the creature cannot easily reach. A Bramble fired in a chain of 2 stays: in the Hawk and Crow fights each firing was worth a tenth of the bar.

Never count pieces for their own sake. A chain of 6 I was offered against the Great Hawk added 0 to the bar and cost 13 health: four of the six pieces were the creature's.

5.6 Passing

  • You may always pass. It costs nothing. The creature and the environment still play. code
  • A pass makes no progress, and every extra round is one more creature turn. Pass only when every move is worse than doing nothing.

5.7 End turn

  • End turn locks the move. There is no undo after it.
  • Then you watch the replay: your chain, the creature's turn, the environment. A Skip button and a speed chip (1x, 2x, Skip) shorten it. not verified

5.8 Guidelines for choosing

  1. Can I win now? If a cell ends the fight, take it. Nothing else matters.
  2. Gain against loss. For each candidate: bar gained, minus health lost in my own chain, minus what the aim does afterwards. Compare shares: 10% of the bar for 10% of my health is a fair trade early and a bad one when I am low.
  3. Free guard first. A Guard placed next to a Hurt both blocks and attacks (Riposte). Firing Rain gives guard worth 15% of your health on top of your chain.
  4. Use what is about to leave. Pieces in their last round, and a Sun or Rain the creature would fire this turn.
  5. Mind the creature's guard and Evade. With "its guard 12" up, a Hurt 3 adds 0: play Pierce or Charm. With "dodges next hit" up, your first Hurt is wasted: lead with a weak Hurt, or play another type.
  6. Do not block for its own sake. Taking the aim cell only makes the creature pick again, at full strength. Block when the aim is unusually big (Sun, a combo, Bramble), or when your piece wants that cell anyway.

5.10 When to flip

  • Flip every piece once while you look. It costs nothing and often opens a cell above or below.
  • A piece with no lit cell at all may have several when flipped. In the Crow fight Brace had "no cells" unflipped and three cells flipped.
  • Flipping never changes the left and right edges. If a piece cannot go beside a neighbour, flipping will not help.

6 The creature's turn

6.1 What happens, in order

  1. Its old guard drops. Guard the creature got on its last turn (or inside your chain) lasted through your turn. Now it is gone, unless a Hold piece keeps it.
  2. Your poison on it ticks. The bar moves. If that fills it, you win before it acts. played
  3. It plays its aim: the piece and the cell it showed you. If it cannot, it picks its best move now.
  4. Its chain fires, by the same rules as yours. A chain of 3 or more uses up what fired.
  5. Extras: a Critter's second piece, one more piece after a Quick piece, a Water creature's push, a Plant's Bramble.
  6. The environment places one piece (chapter 7).
  7. Poison on you ticks. A trip takes effect.
  8. Old pieces leave (3 rounds old). Venom cells fade.
  9. You draw a new hand of 5 (4 if you were tripped). Your guard fades, except what a Hold guard keeps.
  10. The free piece. If the creature has fewer than 2 pieces on the board, the board puts out one for it: its gentlest piece that is not a Hurt, on a random cell where it fits. It does not fire now. played
  11. The new aim is shown.

Code: pfb_foe_turn, pfb_end_round, pfb_spawn, pfb_intent. The replay on the page follows the same order. not verified

6.2 The aim

  • The red mark is the cell. The chip names the piece and what it would do right now: "Peck: hits you for 9".
  • The number is a real dry run of its move: its chain, combos, Sun, your guard, your pieces it fires. code
  • The aim is a promise. The piece lands on that cell even if a use-up has left the cell touching nothing. code

6.3 The creature's pieces

  • The creature holds 3 pieces (a boss 4) and plays one a turn. A boss plays its deck in a fixed order: one set-up piece, then two Hurts, again and again. code
  • The creature must make a link if any of its pieces can. For you, touching and fitting is enough (beside the Blank, for example). code
  • Every creature piece falls away after it has fired twice. The piece view says "plays x2".
  • Its single pieces are small next to yours: the Earwig's 3 against your 5, the Boar's 6 to 7 against your 10 to 16. Most of its damage comes from re-firing an older Hurt with a new one (Double Swing), and from Sun and Bramble.

6.5 Class rules

Every creature has one extra rule from its class. A boss has the boss rule instead. The numbers under "careful player" are from 12 fights per class (24 for Critter, 30 for Boss), so they are rough.

6.5.1Critter: Swarm

Rule text in the game: "It plays two small pieces a turn. The aim shows the first."

How it plays.

  • Each of its pieces counts x0.6. It plays 2 a turn, and a Quick piece adds a third.
  • The aim number covers only the first piece. The second is picked after the first has landed.
  • Two pieces a turn means its chains reach 3 often. It uses up its own pieces, and yours.

Counter-play.

  • Expect about double the chip. Keep some guard up.
  • Bait works well: two placements a turn means two chances to fire your piece. The Earwig fired mine three times in two rounds.
  • Trip and Lull cancel both pieces.
  • Small Critters are the one place where Charm wins fights: gentle ones take Charm at full value.

Careful player: 27% of health lost, 100% wins. 42% of the wins were by charm.

6.5.2Bird: Two aims

Rule text in the game: "It shows two moves on different cells. Take the first cell and it plays the second; take both and it picks again."

How it plays.

  • The second aim can be a different piece. The chip adds it: "or Wing Cover 2 at B4".
  • After you place, the chip shows the total of whichever aim will play ("its second aim").
  • Its base Trick, Flutter Off, gives it Evade whenever it fires.

Counter-play.

  • Taking a cell rarely saves you. Guard does. So does a misfit next to the aim: that breaks it without a second aim.
  • Look at both cells for Sun. In the Crow fight aim 1 stood next to a Sun (8), aim 2 did not.
  • When it "dodges next hit", play Guard, Heal or Charm that turn.

Careful player: 19% of health lost, 100% wins.

6.5.3Beast: Grudge

Rule text in the game: "Each time you block its aim, its Hurt hits 25% harder (up to 3 times). A hit that lands on you clears it."

How it plays.

  • "Block" means: a piece stands on its aim cell when its turn comes. The chip shows "Grudge x1", "x2", "x3".
  • A misfit next to the cell also makes it pick again, and does not add Grudge. code
  • Grudge ends when you lose health on its turn. A hit your guard fully soaks does not clear it.

Counter-play.

  • Let small aims land. A 6 that lands clears the Grudge.
  • Block only the big ones, and prefer breaking the aim with a misfit.
  • Do not sit behind guard for many turns with Grudge x3 up: it never clears, and Bash and Pierce pieces get through.

Careful player: 9% of health lost, 100% wins. The easiest class for a careful player.

6.5.4Water: Current

Rule text in the game: "After its piece lands, one of your pieces touching it slides a cell away, if that cell is free."

How it plays.

  • Only one piece moves, one cell, straight away from the creature’s piece. Checked in the order left, right, above, below.
  • A moved piece keeps its edges. Its old links are gone; it may make new ones where it lands.

Counter-play.

  • Do not count on a link next to the aim cell lasting.
  • A piece with a wall or another piece behind it cannot be pushed.
  • Its Trick, Undertow, trips you: a hand of 4 next round.

Careful player: 32% of health lost, 100% wins.

6.5.5Plant: Grows

Rule text in the game: "After its turn it grows a Bramble beside its piece (one of its own at a time). Whoever chains the Bramble fires it."

How it plays.

  • The Bramble has wild edges: it links to anything next to it.
  • It appears beside the piece the Plant just placed, so the Plant is first in line to fire it next turn: you lose a tenth of your health.
  • Its guard, Bramble Coat, has Thorns. Sap Mend empties the bar.

Counter-play.

  • Fire the Bramble yourself first: a tenth of the bar for you, and in a chain of 2 it stays for another go.
  • Or take the cell that would link its next piece to the Bramble.
  • Hit its guard with Pierce or Charm, not with small Hurts.

Careful player: 21% of health lost, 100% wins, the shortest fights (6.3 turns): its Brambles fed my bar.

6.5.6Reptile: Venom trail

Rule text in the game: "When one of its pieces leaves the board, that cell is venom for 2 turns. A piece of yours put there poisons you (2 a round, 2 rounds)."

How it plays.

  • Venom cells are marked on the board. Any way of leaving counts: use-up, firing twice, old age.
  • Its Trick, Venom Spit, also poisons. Poison is not in the aim number.

Counter-play.

  • Read the mark before you place. The lit number does not show the venom.
  • Stepping on venom can still be right, for a win or a big chain: it costs 4 health in all.
  • Long fights are dangerous here: poison ignores guard.

Careful player: 46% of health lost, 92% wins: the only class that beat my careful player (Sun Lizard, seed 5).

6.5.7Boss: Two turns

Rule text in the game: "It also shows the piece it will play next round. That piece is forced; its cell is chosen then."

How it plays.

  • The chip adds "then Talon Dive 9". The piece is fixed, the cell is not.
  • A boss starts with two pieces, holds 4, cannot be charmed or run from, and wins if the fight reaches 24 turns.
  • Charm still fills its bar at x0.6. The ending is always "backs off".

Counter-play.

  • Use the preview to time your guard: an Evade or Hold guard the turn before its Crush.
  • Its bar is long (215 for the Great Hawk). Sun, Bramble and Poison do the heavy work: one Sun turn of mine was worth 77, plus 44 from poison.
  • Do not block out of habit. It re-picks at full strength.

Careful player: 18% of health lost, 100% wins. Bosses were not harder than large creatures.

7 The environment

7.1 How it works

  • The environment is a third player. After the creature's turn it places one piece. played
  • Its next piece lies face up all round in the NEXT strip. You know what is coming, not where.
  • It puts the piece on an empty cell with nothing on any side, among the three such cells farthest from all pieces. Nothing fires when it lands. code
  • If no lonely cell exists it takes a cell where the piece fits. If 12 or more of the 16 cells are taken it places nothing. code
  • Its pieces belong to nobody. Link one into your chain and it fires for you. The creature can do the same.
  • They count for the chain length and are used up by a chain of 3. Otherwise they stay 3 of your turns.
  • Wind and Mud exist in the code but are not in the deck of a new fight. code

8 Winning and losing

8.1 The hurt route

  • Hurt counts fully against wary creatures and bosses, x0.8 against gentle ones.
  • Combos, Sun, Bramble and Poison all feed the hurt share.
  • It is slowed by the creature's guard, Evade and healing. Pierce answers guard. Nothing answers Evade except not swinging.

8.2 The charm route

  • Charm fills the same bar. Guard, Evade and healing cannot touch it. code
  • It counts x0.6 against wary creatures and bosses. Bond raises that by 0.1 per use, three times at most.
  • You are "won over" only if charm is the larger share when the bar fills. Bramble, Thornburst and Poison count as hurt, so they pull the ending toward "backs off".
  • The reward differs: a charm win gives a gift and the creature's seed or guide drops, but not its ordinary drops. A hurt win gives the ordinary drops. Both give full XP. code

Guideline

Play Charm when the creature is gentle, when its guard is high, or when it dodges your next hit. Do not plan a charm win against a medium or larger creature unless your deck is built for it.

8.3 Bosses

  • A boss cannot be charmed and you cannot run. Its ending is always "backs off".
  • If the fight goes past turn 24 you lose. My longest boss fight took 16 turns (14 for the careful player, whose average was 9.2).
  • Its rule shows you the next round's piece. Its deck order repeats: set-up, Hurt, Hurt.
  • Your Charm pieces are not dead: they add x0.6 to the bar and ignore its guard.

8.4 Health carries over

  • You start the fight with the health your expedition has (at least 1). You leave with what is left. code
  • So a fight won at 20% health makes the next fight on the same trip much harder. Health lost is the real score of a fight.
  • Heal and Item pieces, Mend, and a Rest outside the fight bring it back. A Master Thornblade heals fully after a win. code
  • After a knock-out you wake where you set out, with half your health and 2 energy less. You keep your things. code

8.5 The reward

  • After the last replay a result card shows. One tap, "Collect the reward", ends the fight. not verified
  • XP is the creature's XP, less in an area you have outgrown.
  • Pluck, if it worked, already gave its item during the fight.
  • Tool wear is saved when the fight ends, however it ended.

9.6 Cheat sheet

Goal

  • Fill the Yield bar. Hurt and Charm both fill it.
  • Do not let your health reach 0. It carries over to the next fight.

A round

  • You: one piece (or pass). Flip is free.
  • The creature: its aim, its chain, class extras.
  • The environment: one piece on a lonely cell.
  • Clean-up: poison, old pieces leave, new hand of 5.

Fitting

  • Knob into hole, same family (round, keyhole, triangle).
  • Every touching edge must fit. A fit is a link.
  • Left is a hole, right is a knob. Flip swaps top and bottom.

Chains

  • The placed piece fires first, then linked pieces, newest first.
  • Each piece fires for its owner. Environment pieces fire for the side that started the chain.
  • 3 or more pieces fired: all of them leave the board.
  • Pieces stay 3 rounds. Creature pieces fall after 2 firings.

Combos (your two pieces, one after the other)

  • Trick then Hurt: x1.5 + the Trick's number (Ambush).
  • Guard then Hurt: x1.5 (Riposte).
  • Hurt then Hurt: +2 (Double Swing).
  • Trick then Guard: + the Trick's number (Bait).
  • Place the first piece of the pair. The second must already be on the board.

Before you place: seven checks

  • 1. Can a cell end the fight?
  • 2. Aim chip: how much, where, "fires ...", "2 pieces"?
  • 3. Its chips: guard, dodges next hit, thorny, Grudge.
  • 4. Lit cell: what does it cost me? (creature Hurt, Thornburst, venom)
  • 5. Flipped: is there a better cell?
  • 6. Sun, Rain, Bramble: who fires them first?
  • 7. After placing: what does the aim chip say now?

The aim

  • It lands on its cell unless: your piece is on the cell, a misfit stands beside it, or it is slowed.
  • Then it picks again at full strength. No free turn.
  • The number leaves out a second piece (Swarm, Quick) and poison.
  • A fitting piece of yours beside the aim cell gets fired, for you (bait).

Environment

  • Sun: the chain's owner counts double.
  • Bramble: the other side loses a tenth. Fire it in a chain of 2 to keep it.
  • Rain: guard for whoever fires it. Do not leave it to the creature.
  • Thornburst: hurts whoever fires it. Stay off it.

Class rules

  • Critter: two pieces a turn. Expect double.
  • Bird: two aims. Guard instead of chasing.
  • Beast: blocking makes it hit harder. Let small hits land.
  • Water: pushes your piece away.
  • Plant: grows a Bramble. Fire it first.
  • Reptile: venom cells and poison. Finish fast.
  • Boss: shows next round's piece. No charm win, no running, 24 turns.

Do not

  • Take the biggest number without reading the cost.
  • Count chain length. Count bar and health.
  • Swing into "dodges next hit". Hit "its guard" with small Hurts.
  • Heal at full health. Pass when a free move exists.

Numbers

  • Board 4 by 4. Hand 5. One action. Fight limit 24 turns.
  • Charm x0.6 on wary creatures and bosses. Hurt x0.8 on gentle ones.
  • Sun x2. Bramble 10%. Rain 15%. Thornburst 10% (you), 5% (it).
  • Grudge +25% a block, up to 3. Venom 2 a round for 2 rounds.

10 Open questions and confusing places

These are the places where the rules, the texts or the numbers did not agree while I played, or where a rule works but will surprise a player. Each has a code place or a fight you can replay with php tests/pf_play.php new STATE SLUG LEVEL SEED v1. They are ordered by how much they hurt a player.

10.1 The aim number leaves out the second piece

  • The chip is a dry run of the first piece only (pfb_aim_total: "Quick's extra piece, the poison ticks and the terrain turn are not in the numbers").
  • Sun Lizard, level 8, seed 5, careful policy, turn 2: the chip read 3 after my placement. Lizard Dart is Quick. It then played Venom Spit, which re-fired Lizard Dart with Ambush under a Sun. I lost 22 of 44 health in that round, and the fight.
  • Carrion Crow, seed 5, turn 2: the chip read 9 with "Quick: plays again". My guard of 7 left 2. The extra piece then cost 9 more.
  • Over all careful fights the loss was larger than the chip on 20% to 51% of turns (6.2). For a Critter the second piece comes every turn.
  • Question: should the chip show a range, or run the second piece too? "2 pieces" does not tell a player that the hit may triple.

10.2 Quick on your own pieces does nothing

  • Text: "Quick: draws you a piece when you place it" (PF_TAGS_V1).
  • Rule: one action a turn, and the whole hand goes to the discard at the end of the round (pfb_end_round, redraw 1). The drawn piece can never be played.
  • This touches Jab (in every ready kit), Dibber Poke and Pickle Splash. The "Sticky" upgrade draws a piece in the same dead way. code
  • For the creature, Quick is strong: one more piece that turn.
  • Question: what should Quick do for the player now? A second action would be a new rule.

10.3 "Stays N rounds" is printed in three places and is wrong in two

  • Per-piece life is off ('life' => 0 in pfb_v1_cfg), so pfb_life returns 3 for every piece.
  • The NEXT strip still reads the data file: "Sun, stays 2 rounds" (pfb_view, env.next.life). In play the Sun and the Rain then showed "stays 3" on the board and did stay 3 turns (Crow, seed 5: Sun placed in round 1, expired at the end of round 4).
  • The creature card prints "stays 2 rounds" for Skitter (pfv_foe_card), and the data file comment says Quick pieces last 2 and Hold pieces 4.
  • The piece card in the hand prints "stays 3" from a default, which happens to be right.

10.4 The help sheet explains a rule that is off

  • Aim strike has been off in new fights since 2.6.984 (aimhit 0).
  • The help row "The creature's aim" always says: "If its piece is a Hurt and you take the cell with one of YOUR pieces, it strikes that piece instead: the piece breaks" (tools/pfboardv1_part.js, helpSheet; the sentence is not behind the switch).
  • A player who reads the help will not dare to take the aim cell.

10.5 Trip and Lull remove a whole creature turn

  • The creature plays one piece a turn, so "one piece fewer" is none (pfb_foe_len). The text now says so: "the creature skips its next turn".
  • It applies the same round, to a Critter's two pieces and to a boss.
  • These are cheap pieces (Trip, Net Toss: Hurt base 2; Lull, Glare: Charm base 3). Against the Great Hawk, Lull offered "+8.4 and it loses the turn".
  • The creature's Trip does something quite different to you: you draw 4 instead of 5. One word, two effects.
  • Question: is a full skip the intended strength?

10.6 Poison follows the boosted hit, and works through Evade

  • Each tick is half of the piece's fired value, after combos and Sun (pf_apply: ceil($val / 2)).
  • Great Hawk, seed 9, turn 4: Pepper Puff 16 under Ambush and Sun fired for 88. The tick was 44. That tick ended a 215-point boss fight on turn 4.
  • Turn 3 of the same fight: the Hawk dodged the hit (0 damage), but the poison was applied anyway: 16 a tick.
  • The text says only "hits again each of the next 2 turns". How much is not said. A new Poison replaces the old ticks instead of adding to them, which the text does not say either.

10.7 Rain is not the same piece for both sides

  • Your Rain guard is 15% of your health. The creature's is 15% of its Yield bar (pfb_env_fire).
  • Earwig: 5 guard for me, 6 for it, against my Hurt pieces of 3 to 5. With its own guards it stood at 12, and my Jab read 0.
  • Wild Boar: 13 for me, 26 for it, against my Hurt pieces of 10 to 16.
  • Its guard also soaks every Hurt in my chain until its next turn. Only Pierce and Charm get past.
  • The help text says "it gets the guard" but not how much. The ring badge does show the number.

10.8 Grudge: what counts as a block

  • Rule text: "Each time you block its aim".
  • Code: Grudge goes up only when a piece stands on the aim cell (pfb_foe_turn). Breaking the aim with a misfit beside the cell makes it pick again and adds no Grudge.
  • If your chain of 3 uses up the piece you put on the cell, the cell is free again, the aim lands, and there is no Grudge (Boar, seed 3, turn 2).
  • Grudge also raises a Beast Hurt that fires inside your chain.
  • "A hit that lands" means you lost health during its turn. A fully guarded hit keeps the Grudge.

10.9 Hidden multipliers

  • A Charm 5 adds 3 to the bar of a wary creature. A Hurt 10 adds 8 to a gentle one. The piece shows 5 and 10.
  • The lit cell shows the true number, so the page is honest at the moment of choice. The deck screen is not: there Charm looks as strong as Hurt.
  • Bond does nothing against a gentle creature (Charm is already x1).

10.10 Things a new player will not guess

  • Trick order. "Boosts a Hurt or Guard fired right after it" is true, but only if you place the Trick. Nothing says so.
  • Bait. The help says "If its aim fires one of your pieces, your piece works for you". In play this is one of the strongest moves, and the creature cannot avoid it, because its aim is fixed before you move. Is that intended?
  • Evade eats bait. A creature Guard or Trick with Evade that fires earlier in its chain makes your baited Hurt miss (Crow, seed 5, turn 7).
  • Net health on a lit cell. A heal in the same chain hides a Thornburst or a creature hit: the cell shows the net (the harness note says "h-0 can hide a loss").
  • The creature finishing itself. When its chain fires a Thornburst the bar grows by 5%. The aim total does not count that as "to it". The Crow fight ended this way.
  • Heavy is a tag with no effect on this board.
  • The free piece. The board gives the creature a piece when it has fewer than 2 out. It looks like a second move. The replay names it since 2.6.97.

10.11 The board does not last

  • At the start of my turn I had no piece of my own on the board in 51% of turns (careful) to 68% (longest chain).
  • Use-up, two firings, three rounds and the full redraw all clear things away. Most turns are a fresh puzzle: which hand piece links to what the creature and the environment left.
  • This is known (the T15 bake-off). I note it because several guidelines a rule book would like to give ("build a wall", "keep your trick for later") are not possible in the game as it stands.

10.12 Charm is a small-creature route

  • Charm endings: 56% small, 6% medium, 3% large, 0% boss for the careful player (8.2).
  • Bramble, Thornburst and Poison count as hurt, so the environment pushes every long fight toward "backs off".
  • The reward for a charm win is different (gift, seed, guide). A player who wants it has little control above small size.

10.13 Smaller text slips

  • tests/pf_play.php footer still says: "a flip is free, once a turn", "A piece must make a linked joint unless nothing can", and lists Teamwork. All three are off. Testers who read it play by the old rules.
  • The same harness prints "on side ?" for both start pieces when the Blank is not in the centre (it works the side out from the centre cell).
  • The harness lets a Snack be placed at any health. The game refuses it at full health and after the snack limit (pfight_game.php). Bots that use the harness overrate Snack.
  • The harness does not print the creature's guard, its Evade, the second aim, Grudge or venom cells. The page does. I read them from the state file.
  • PFB_SUN_MULT is commented "a Hurt or Charm piece"; the Sun piece doubles every type, as the help text says.
  • The page names cells A1 to D4 (column letter, row number: "or Wing Cover 2 at B4"). The harness and this book count x,y from 0. Cell 2,1 here is C2 on the page.

10.14 What I could not verify

  • The live page: layout, taps, replay timing, the look of chips and rings. All page statements come from the source files.
  • Rewards, knock-out and health carry-over: read in adventure.php and pfight_game.php, not played.
  • The Puzzle box, belt and pre-fight screens.
  • Real decks. My gnome is the test gnome of the harness, at the creature's level.
  • The JS twin (tools/pfengine.js). I read its header and constants only; all play was on the PHP engine.
  • Bait, Calm Charm and Win Over combos, Bash, Thorns, Mend, Bond and Pluck: read in code, not seen firing.
