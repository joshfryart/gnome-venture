1 What you are doing

Puzzle combat is a small board game inside Gnome Venture. You meet a creature. You both put jigsaw pieces on a 4 by 4 board. Pieces that link fire together as a chain. This book describes the rules as they are in a new fight today (version 2.7.00, puzzle combat v1, still switched on for admins only: setting pfight_v1 Off / Admins only). Fights already saved keep the rules they started with (PFB_V1_PRE).

1.1 The goal

Fill the creature's Yield bar before your health reaches 0. That is the whole game. played

1.2 The Yield bar

  • The creature has one bar. It starts empty, for example Yield 0/43.
  • Your Hurt pieces fill it. Your Charm pieces fill the same bar. played
  • When it is full the creature yields and you win.
  • The label counts both parts: "Yield 23/40: hurt 15, charm 8". code
  • The bar can go down: a creature's heal empties it, the hurt part first, then the charm part (new in 2.6.989). code

1.3 Your health

  • Your health is the health of your expedition. You bring it into the fight and you take what is left out of it. code
  • The creature's Hurt pieces lower it. So do Bramble, Thornburst and poison.
  • Guard blocks the creature's Hurt pieces. Guard does not block Bramble played, Thornburst or poison code.
  • At 0 you are knocked out and the fight is lost.

1.4 One round

Figure 1. A round. You act once, then you watch.
  • You do one action: place one piece, or pass. Flipping a piece is free and is not an action.
  • Then the creature plays. Then the environment places one piece. Then old pieces leave and you draw a new hand.
  • Your hand is thrown away and redrawn every round. You cannot save a piece for later, with one exception: a piece with the Keeper tag stays in your hand (new in 2.6.990, see 2.9). played

Every piece has a name, a type, a number, four edges and sometimes a tag. A legendary crop piece can also carry a built-in knack (2.11).

2.3 Edges, fitting and linking

  • Each edge is a knob (solid, sticks out) or a hole (hollow, cut in).
  • Each knob and hole belongs to a family: body (round), mind (keyhole) or craft (triangle).
  • Two edges fit when one is a knob and the other a hole of the same family.
  • You may place a piece on an empty cell only if it touches at least one piece and every edge that touches a piece fits. played
  • A joint that fits is a link. Chains run along links.
Figure 3. Fitting. There is no "almost": a touching edge fits or the cell is closed to that piece.

Two special edges exist:

  • Wild: fits anything. The Blank has four wild edges, but no chain runs through the Blank. A Plant's own Bramble also has wild edges, and it does link. code
  • Flat: fits anything and never links. Today only Offer (flat right edge) has one. Mud, which is out of the game for now, is flat all round. code

You do not need to learn this by heart, the lit cells show where a piece goes. But the pattern helps you plan:

The top and bottom family follows where the piece comes from: tool and craft pieces craft, pal pieces body, crop pieces and legendary crop pieces mind, all others the family of their type (PFB_SRC_FAM, pfb_author_edges). Checked against every hand I was dealt, the legendary pieces included. played

What follows from it: a Hurt or a Guard can sit to the right of any piece with a body knob on its right. Pieces of the same column family stack above and below each other, knob-on-top under hole-on-top.

A legendary Heal piece has mind edges on three sides (left, top, bottom). It only goes beside mind knobs. In one fight I held Pecan Pie and Buckthorn Zing with no cell at all, flipped or not. Beside the Blank, in round 1, they always fit. played

2.4 Flipping

Figure 4. Flip.
  • Flip is free and unlimited. Flip the piece, look at the new lit cells, flip it back if you like. played
  • A flipped piece stays flipped on the board. It is the right way up again when it comes back into your hand. A Keeper piece that you flipped and did not play stays flipped in your hand next round. played
  • Flip matters when the only free cell next to your target is on the wrong side. In my Carrion Crow fight, turn 6, the flipped Snack reached a cell beside a Sun and healed 13. Unflipped, its two cells both ran into a Thornburst. played

The order rule that decides most turns

The piece you place fires first. So to get Ambush you place the Trick next to a Hurt that is already on the board. To get Riposte you place the Guard next to a Hurt that is already there. Placing the Hurt next to the Trick gives no combo at all.

2.8 Where the numbers come from

  • A piece's number is its base value, plus your stat for that type, plus a third of your gnome level (rounded down), plus a rank bonus (Uncommon +2, Rare +4, Masterwork +5). code
  • Stats by type: Hurt uses Grit, Guard Nimble, Heal Hardiness, Charm and Soothe Charm, Trick and Item Lore. code
  • A legendary crop piece takes its rank from your mastery of that one crop (3.7). code
  • A tool piece wears its tool: 2 durability each time you play it. A chip in the fight shows the wear. code

3 Setting up a fight

Everything in this chapter is read from the page code (pfdeck.php, pfdeck_view.php). I did not open the live pages. not verified The deck builder itself (pfd_build) I ran with made-up gnomes (play/checks.php); where a line rests on that, it says so.

3.1 The Puzzle box and your deck

  • Your deck is built in the Puzzle box. It has three tabs: Kit, Belt, Pals.
  • The first time, you pack a starter: 4 fixed pieces (Swing, Dodge, Rest, Charm) plus 6 of 12 offered pieces. One tap picks a ready kit instead:
    • Bold (the default): your calling piece, Jab, a second Swing, Pierce, Snack, Oat Cake.
    • Steady: calling piece, Jab, second Swing, Pierce, Brace, your home region's piece.
    • Kind: calling piece, Jab, second Swing, Pierce, Hum, Soothe.
  • Later you switch on more pieces you own: knacks (by level), crop pieces, legendary crop pieces, craft pieces, region pieces.
  • New in 2.6.989: a piece you have just earned arrives switched off, with a NEW mark, until you switch it once. Before, crop, craft and region pieces arrived switched on. A gnome who already owned such pieces finds them marked NEW and off after the update. (Run: a gnome with a new crop piece and two new legendary pieces got all three "off, NEW".)
  • Limits: at least 8 kit pieces, at least 3 Hurt pieces in a custom starter, at least 1 Hurt piece in any deck, at most 3 Rare and 1 Masterwork (a Fine wearable counts as Rare, see 3.8), and the slots of 3.6. There is no maximum size.
  • If the deck as switched is not legal (under 8 kit pieces or no Hurt), the fight plays the default kit instead.
  • The deck bar shows "Hurt pieces: N of M". Under 3 in 10 it warns: "Few Hurt pieces: fights will be long".
  • The Kit tab has filter buttons: All, basic, crop, a crown for legendary crop pieces (new in 2.6.990), craft, region.

3.2 The belt

  • Each tool on your fight belt adds its one piece to the deck (axe: Chop; tilling tool: Hoe Hook; staff: Sweep; lantern: Glare; and so on).
  • Belt slots: 2 for every gnome, 3 with the Stitched Fight Belt (level 8), 4 with the Masterwork Fight Belt (level 18).
  • A tool has 20 durability, plus 4 per rank. Each play of its piece costs 2. A broken tool gives no piece. Fix it with a Tool Repair Kit, or Remove it from the belt.
  • Tool pieces are not slotted beyond the belt: they need no Larder or Keepsake slot.

3.3 Pals

Pals in your party lend one piece each (for example Bark Badger, a Guard; Samara Spin, a Hurt). Pal pieces have body tops and bottoms.

3.4 The pre-fight screen

  • When a creature stands in front of you, you see its creature card, your deck and your health, and the button Let's fight.
  • With Auto deck on, the deck is locked and you only press the button. With it off, tap a deck piece to take it out or a bench piece to add it.
  • "Nothing changes once the fight starts." The deck is locked during the fight.

What it means when a creature piece is in your chain

  • Its Hurt hits you. Its Guard gives the creature guard that soaks your later Hurt. Its Heal empties the bar. Its tag works for it (Evade, Thorns, Poison, Trip).
  • A Critter's Hurt gets its Swarm bonus there too.
  • But it also spends one of that piece's two firings. A creature piece that has fired twice falls off the board.
  • So firing a harmless creature piece (a Trick with no tag, a small Guard when you play Charm or Pierce) is a cheap way to remove it. Against a Critter it also shrinks the Swarm.

Bait: what it means when your piece is in the creature's chain

  • Your piece fires for you, with your combos, in the creature's turn. played
  • The aim is fixed before you move. If you put a piece next to the aim cell with an edge that fits, the creature's piece will link to it and fire it. An environment piece between the two works as a bridge (figure 8).
  • In the Great Hawk fight the creature's own chain gave me 19, in the Wild Boar fight 11.
  • The aim chip shows it after you place: "fires your Pierce: 11 to it". code
  • It works for Heal pieces too: the Crow's chain fired my Snack and healed me 4. played
  • Bait fails when the creature has Evade up or gets it earlier in that chain: your Hurt misses. code

5.5 The chain of 3 rule (use-up)

  • A chain that fires 3 or more pieces uses up every piece that fired. They leave the board at once. played
  • It does not matter who started the chain or who owns the pieces. Environment pieces count and are used up too.
  • A chain of 1 or 2 uses nothing up. The pieces stay and can fire again next turn.
  • Used-up pieces of yours go to your discard pile. You will draw them again later.

When is a chain of 3 worth it?

Usually yes. The board empties all the time anyway: in my careful runs I had no piece of my own on the board at the start of 50% of my turns. A piece you "save" is often used up by the creature's chain, or it expires. Take the 3-chain when one of these is true:

  • It adds clearly more to the bar than the best chain of 2. In the Boar fight, turn 2: 30.6 against 15.
  • Your pieces in it are in their last round ("leaves this round").
  • It uses up a creature piece you want gone (a Hurt next to the aim, an Evade piece, a Critter's extra pieces).
  • It uses up a Rain or a Sun before the creature fires it. In the Hawk fight, turn 3, that kept 32 guard out of its hands.
  • It ends the fight.

Keep the chain at 2 when it fires a Bramble, Sun or Rain that you can fire again next turn and the creature cannot easily reach. A Bramble fired in a chain of 2 stays: in the Crow fight I fired the same Bramble on turns 7 and 9, a tenth of the bar each time.

Never count pieces for their own sake. A chain of 6 I was offered against the Carrion Crow (turn 2) added 0 to the bar: three of the six pieces were the creature's or the environment's, and the one Hurt in it missed.

  • A pass keeps a Keeper piece, like any other round.

5.8 Guidelines for choosing

  1. Can I win now? If a cell ends the fight, take it. Nothing else matters.
  2. Gain against loss. For each candidate: bar gained, minus health lost in my own chain, minus what the aim does afterwards. Compare shares: 10% of the bar for 10% of my health is a fair trade early and a bad one when I am low.
  3. Free guard first. A Guard placed next to a Hurt both blocks and attacks (Riposte). Firing Rain gives guard worth 15% of your health on top of your chain.
  4. Use what is about to leave. Pieces in their last round, and a Sun or Rain the creature would fire this turn.
  5. Mind the creature's guard and Evade. With "its guard 38" up (Great Hawk after a Rain), my Swing 15 and Chop 19 read 0 and only the Pierce piece counted: play Pierce or Charm. With "dodges next hit" up, your first Hurt is wasted: lead with a weak Hurt, or play another type.
  6. Do not block for its own sake. Taking the aim cell only makes the creature pick again, at full strength. Block when the aim is unusually big (Sun, a combo, Bramble), or when your piece wants that cell anyway.
  7. Against a Critter, count its pieces. Two others out: +1 on each of its Hurts. Four: +2. A chain that uses its pieces up is worth more than its bar number says.

6.5.1Critter: Swarm

Rule text in the game: "Its Hurt hits +1 for every 2 of its other pieces on the board (up to +2). Use up or outlast its pieces to shrink it."

How it plays.

  • New rule since 2.6.988. A Critter plays one piece a turn, like everyone else, and its pieces have their full number (the old rule was two pieces a turn at x0.6).
  • When one of its Hurt pieces fires, count its other pieces on the board at that moment: 2 or 3 give +1, 4 or more give +2. Guards, Tricks, the free piece and the start piece all count. code (run: a Hurt 4 fired for 4, 4, 5, 5, 6, 6, 6 with 0 to 6 others)
  • The bonus is added to every Hurt in its chain, after its combo and before a Sun doubles it. It is in the aim number. played
  • It also applies when its Hurt fires inside your chain. code
  • Its base Trick, Skitter, is Quick: that turn it still plays a second piece.

Counter-play.

  • Count its pieces before you read the aim. With two others out, a Double Swing of two Hurts printed 5 and 4 read 13.

  • Taking a cell rarely saves you, and it can cost you: in the Crow fight a piece on aim 1 would have turned a 3 into a 9 (figure 10). Guard helps. So does a misfit next to the aim: that breaks it without a second aim.

6.5.3Beast: Grudge

Rule text in the game: "Each time you block its aim, its Hurt hits 25% harder (up to 3 times). A hit that lands on you clears it."

How it plays.

  • "Block" means: a piece stands on its aim cell when its turn comes. The chip shows "Grudge x1", "x2", "x3".
  • A misfit next to the cell also makes it pick again, and does not add Grudge. code
  • Grudge ends when you lose health on its turn. A hit your guard fully soaks does not clear it. (Boar, turn 5: the 8 was blocked, Grudge x1 stayed.) played

Counter-play.

  • Let small aims land. A 6 that lands clears the Grudge.
  • Block only the big ones, and prefer breaking the aim with a misfit.
  • Do not sit behind guard for many turns with Grudge x3 up: it never clears, and Bash and Pierce pieces get through.

Careful player: 24% of health lost, 100% wins.

6.5.4Water: Current

Rule text in the game: "After its piece lands, one of your pieces touching it slides a cell away, if that cell is free."

How it plays.

  • Only one piece moves, one cell, straight away from the creature’s piece. Checked in the order left, right, above, below. code
  • A moved piece keeps its edges. Its old links are gone; it may make new ones where it lands.

Counter-play.

  • Do not count on a link next to the aim cell lasting.
  • A piece with a wall or another piece behind it cannot be pushed.
  • Its Trick, Undertow, trips you: a hand of 4 next round.

6.5.5Plant: Grows

Rule text in the game: "After its turn it grows a Bramble beside its piece (one of its own at a time). Whoever chains the Bramble fires it."

How it plays.

  • The Bramble has wild edges: it links to anything next to it. code
  • It appears beside the piece the Plant just placed, so the Plant is first in line to fire it next turn: you lose a tenth of your health.
  • Its guard, Bramble Coat, has Thorns. Sap Mend empties the bar: the hurt part first, then your charm.

Counter-play.

  • Fire the Bramble yourself first: a tenth of the bar for you, and in a chain of 2 it stays for another go.
  • Or take the cell that would link its next piece to the Bramble.
  • Hit its guard with Pierce or Charm, not with small Hurts.

Careful player: 19% of health lost, 100% wins, the shortest fights (6.2 turns): its Brambles fed my bar.

6.5.6Reptile: Venom trail

Rule text in the game: "When one of its pieces leaves the board, that cell is venom for 2 turns. A piece of yours put there poisons you (2 a round, 2 rounds)."

How it plays.

  • Venom cells are marked on the board. Any way of leaving counts: use-up, firing twice, old age. code
  • Its Trick, Venom Spit, also poisons. Poison is not in the aim number.

6.5.7Boss: Two turns

Rule text in the game: "It also shows the piece it will play next round. That piece is forced; its cell is chosen then."

How it plays.

  • The chip adds "then Talon Dive 9". The piece is fixed, the cell is not. In the Hawk fight every preview came true. played
  • A boss starts with two pieces, holds 4, cannot be charmed or run from, and wins if the fight reaches 24 turns.

7 The environment

7.1 How it works

  • The environment is a third player. After the creature's turn it places one piece. played
  • Its next piece lies face up all round in the NEXT strip. You know what is coming, not where.
  • It puts the piece on an empty cell with nothing on any side, among the three such cells farthest from all pieces. Nothing fires when it lands. code
  • If no lonely cell exists it takes a cell where the piece fits. If 12 or more of the 16 cells are taken it places nothing. code
  • Its pieces belong to nobody. Link one into your chain and it fires for you. The creature can do the same. played
  • They count for the chain length and are used up by a chain of 3. Otherwise they stay 3 of your turns. played

8 Winning and losing

8.1 The hurt route

  • Hurt counts fully against wary creatures and bosses, x0.8 against gentle ones.
  • Combos, Sun, Bramble and Poison all feed the hurt share.
  • It is slowed by the creature's guard, Evade and healing. Pierce answers guard. Nothing answers Evade except not swinging.

8.2 The charm route

  • Charm fills the same bar. Guard and Evade cannot touch it. played
  • Healing can, since 2.6.989: a creature's heal empties the hurt part first and then the charm part. A bar filled only by Charm is no longer safe from Sap Mend and other heal pieces. code
  • It counts x0.6 against wary creatures and bosses. Bond raises that by 0.1 per use, three times at most.
  • You are "won over" only if charm is the larger share when the bar fills. Bramble, Thornburst and Poison count as hurt, so they pull the ending toward "backs off".
  • The reward differs: a charm win gives a gift and the creature's seed or guide drops, but not its ordinary drops. A hurt win gives the ordinary drops. Both give full XP. code

Guideline

Play Charm when the creature is gentle, when its guard is high, or when it dodges your next hit. Do not plan a charm win against a medium or larger creature unless your deck is built for it.

8.3 Bosses

  • A boss cannot be charmed and you cannot run. Its ending is always "backs off".
  • If the fight goes past turn 24 you lose. My longest boss fight took 15 turns (13 for the careful player, whose average was 8.7).
  • Its rule shows you the next round's piece. Its deck order repeats: set-up, Hurt, Hurt.
  • Your Charm pieces are not dead: they add x0.6 to the bar and ignore its guard. In the Hawk fight Charm pieces added 43 of the 232 I scored.

8.4 Health carries over

  • You start the fight with the health your expedition has (at least 1). You leave with what is left. code
  • So a fight won at 20% health makes the next fight on the same trip much harder. Health lost is the real score of a fight.
  • Heal and Item pieces, Mend, Simmer, Leech and a Rest outside the fight bring it back. A Master Thornblade heals fully after a win. code
  • After a knock-out you wake where you set out, with half your health and 2 energy less. You keep your things. code

8.5 The reward

  • After the last replay a result card shows. One tap, "Collect the reward", ends the fight. not verified
  • XP is the creature's XP, less in an area you have outgrown.
  • Pluck, if it worked, already gave its item during the fight.
  • Tool wear is saved when the fight ends, however it ended.

9.6 Cheat sheet

Goal

  • Fill the Yield bar. Hurt and Charm both fill it. Its heal empties it.
  • Do not let your health reach 0. It carries over to the next fight.

A round

  • You: one piece (or pass). Flip is free.
  • The creature: its aim, its chain, class extras.
  • The environment: one piece on a lonely cell.
  • Clean-up: poison, Simmer, old pieces leave, new hand of 5.

Fitting

  • Knob into hole, same family (round, keyhole, triangle).
  • Every touching edge must fit. A fit is a link.
  • Left is a hole, right is a knob. Flip swaps top and bottom.

Chains

  • The chain is the placed piece and what links to it, newest first. It fires in rank order: environment, Guard / Soothe, Tricks, Hurt / Charm, healing.
  • Each piece fires for its owner. Environment pieces fire for the side that started the chain.
  • 3 or more pieces fired: all of them leave the board.
  • Pieces stay 3 rounds. Creature pieces fall after 2 firings.

Combos (your two pieces, one after the other)

  • Trick then Hurt: x1.5 + the Trick's number (Ambush).
  • Guard then Hurt: x1.5 (Riposte).
  • Hurt then Hurt: +2 (Double Swing).
  • Trick then Guard: + the Trick's number (Bait).
  • Place the first piece of the pair. The second must already be on the board.

Before you place: seven checks

  • 1. Can a cell end the fight?
  • 2. Aim chip: how much, where, "fires ..."? Poison on top?
  • 3. Its chips: guard, dodges next hit, thorny, Grudge, Swarm.
  • 4. Lit cell: what does it cost me? (creature Hurt, Thornburst, venom)
  • 5. Flipped: is there a better cell?
  • 6. Sun, Rain, Bramble: who fires them first?
  • 7. After placing: what does the aim chip say now?

The aim

  • It lands on its cell unless: your piece is on the cell, a misfit stands beside it, or it is slowed.
  • Then it picks again at full strength. No free turn.
  • The number is its whole turn. It leaves out poison and venom.
  • A fitting piece of yours beside the aim cell gets fired, for you (bait).

Environment

  • Sun: the chain's owner counts double.
  • Bramble: the other side loses a tenth. Fire it in a chain of 2 to keep it.
  • Rain: guard for whoever fires it. Do not leave it to the creature.
  • Thornburst: hurts whoever fires it. Stay off it.

Class rules

  • Critter: its Hurt +1 per 2 other pieces it has out (max +2).
  • Bird: two aims. Guard instead of chasing.
  • Beast: blocking makes it hit harder. Let small hits land.
  • Water: pushes your piece away.
  • Plant: grows a Bramble. Fire it first.
  • Reptile: venom cells and poison. Finish fast.
  • Boss: shows next round's piece. No charm win, no running, 24 turns.

New tags and slots

  • Keeper: stays in your hand until played.
  • Plenty: next round a hand of 6. Simmer: heal 2, twice.
  • Poison ticks are flat: half the printed number, 2 turns.
  • New pieces arrive off, marked NEW. Larder: 3 crop pieces (4 at level 10, 5 at 20).

Do not

  • Take the biggest number without reading the cost.
  • Count chain length. Count bar and health.
  • Swing into "dodges next hit". Hit "its guard" with small Hurts.
  • Heal at full health. Pass when a free move exists.

Numbers

  • Board 4 by 4. Hand 5. One action. Fight limit 24 turns.
  • Charm x0.6 on wary creatures and bosses. Hurt x0.8 on gentle ones.
  • Sun x2. Bramble 10%. Rain 15%. Thornburst 10% (you), 5% (it).
  • Grudge +25% a block, up to 3. Venom 2 a round for 2 rounds. Poison on you: at most 3 ticks.

10.18 Keeper, Plenty and Simmer: the small printnew

  • Keeper narrows the draw. The hand is filled up to 5, so a kept piece costs one new piece every round it stays. The text "stays in your hand until you play it" does not say that. Two Keepers in hand leave 3 new pieces a round.
  • Keeper keeps its flip. Every other piece comes back the right way up; a kept piece stays as you left it. Fine, but the only place a flip carries over.
  • Only when placed. Plenty and Simmer do nothing when a later chain fires the piece again (pf_apply: the root piece only). The piece's own Heal does fire again. So the same piece gives its tag once and its number several times.
  • No chip while they run. The page has chips for poison, trip and Evade, but none for "Simmer: 1 more" or "Plenty: 6 next round" (tools/pfboardv1_part.js has no such text). You find out when it happens.
  • Simmer at full health heals 0 and the tick is spent. It is also the last thing in the clean-up, after the poison tick, so it cannot save you from that tick.
  • A second Simmer does not add: the count is set to 2, not raised by 2. Plenty is a flag: two in a round give one extra piece. The Plenty text says "pick from 6", the changelog says "does not stack"; the Simmer text says nothing.
  • Plenty and a trip: 5 pieces, not 6. Plenty and a Keeper: 6 with the Keeper among them.
  • The harness cannot deal them (10.13), so neither the bots nor a tester on the plain harness has ever played one.
