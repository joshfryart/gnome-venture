Puzzle combat in one minute

  • You and a creature take turns putting jigsaw pieces on a 4 by 4 board.
  • Pieces whose edges fit are linked. When you place a piece, it and every piece linked to it fire together as a chain.
  • Your Hurt and Charm pieces fill the creature's Yield bar. Fill it and you win.
  • The creature's Hurt pieces lower your health. At 0 you lose.
  • You see where the creature will play next (its aim) before you move. Use that.
  • A third player, the environment, drops Sun, Rain, Bramble and Thornburst pieces. Whoever links them in gets the effect.

Saved fights

A fight that was already saved before 2.7.00 keeps its old rules: the chain fires in link order, and the creature gets a free piece when it has fewer than 2 on the board. This book describes new fights.

1 Goal and the Yield bar

Fill the creature's Yield bar before your health reaches 0. That is the whole game.

  • The creature has one bar. It starts empty, for example "Yield 0/43".
  • Your Hurt pieces fill it. Your Charm pieces fill the same bar. The label shows both: "Yield 23/40: hurt 15, charm 8".
  • The bar can go down: a creature's heal empties it, the hurt part first, then the charm part.
  • Bigger and higher-level creatures have longer bars.

A piece does not always add its printed number. The creature card tells you its temper:

CreatureHurt 10 addsCharm 10 addsThe card says
Wary (most creatures)106"wary"
Gentle810"gentle"
Boss106"cannot be charmed" (Charm still fills the bar)

Your health

  • You bring your expedition's health into the fight, and you leave with what is left. Health lost is the real score of a fight.
  • The creature's Hurt pieces lower it. So do Bramble, Thornburst and poison.
  • Guard blocks the creature's Hurt pieces. Guard does not block Bramble, Thornburst or poison.

If one chain fills the bar and takes your last health, you win: the bar is checked first.

2 One round

Figure 1. A round. You act once, then you watch.
  1. You do one action: place one piece, or pass. Flipping is free and is not an action.
  2. The creature plays the piece it aimed, and its chain fires.
  3. The environment places one piece on an empty cell. Nothing fires.
  4. Clean-up: poison ticks, old pieces leave, and you get a new hand of 5.

Your turn in taps

  1. Look. Read the Yield bar, the aim chip and its red mark, the creature's status chips, the environment pieces and your hand.
  2. Pick. Tap a hand piece. The cells where it may go light up. Each lit cell shows what the chain adds to the bar and what it costs you.
  3. Flip. Tap Flip and look again. Every piece has two shapes.
  4. Place. Tap a lit cell. The plan line shows your chain and the result. The aim chip updates. Not happy? Pick again.
  5. End turn. This locks the move. There is no undo. Then you watch the replay.

Your hand

  • Your hand is thrown away and redrawn every round. You cannot save a piece, except a Keeper piece (chapter 4).
  • Used and expired pieces go to your discard pile. You draw them again later.
  • You may always pass. It costs nothing, but the creature and the environment still play. Pass only when every move is worse than nothing.

3 The pieces

Every piece has a name, a type, a number, four edges and sometimes a tag. A legendary crop piece can also carry a built-in knack.

Fitting and linking

  • Each edge is a knob (solid, sticks out) or a hole (hollow, cut in).
  • Each knob and hole has a family: body (round), mind (keyhole) or craft (triangle).
  • Two edges fit when one is a knob and the other a hole of the same family.
  • You may place a piece on an empty cell only if it touches at least one piece and every touching edge fits. There is no "almost".
  • A joint that fits is a link. Chains run along links.
Figure 3. Fitting.

Special edges. Wild fits anything. The Blank has four wild edges, but no chain runs through it. A Plant's Bramble also has wild edges, and it does link. Flat fits anything and never links (today only the right edge of Offer).

How your pieces are cut

The lit cells show you where a piece goes, so you need not learn this. It helps you plan.

Flipping

  • Flip swaps the top and bottom edges. Left and right never change. If a piece cannot go beside a neighbour, flipping will not help.
  • Flip is free and unlimited. Flip every piece once while you look: it often opens a cell above or below.
  • A flipped piece stays flipped on the board. It comes back into your hand the right way up (a Keeper piece you did not play keeps its flip).

Keeper, Plenty and Simmer in practice

  • They work only when you place the piece. If a later chain fires it again, its number fires but the tag does nothing.
  • Keeper is the only way to plan ahead. Keep the Guard for the round the aim is big, or for a Riposte. But a kept piece costs you one new piece each round.
  • Plenty pays next round. Place it when this round's best move is small anyway.
  • Simmer is worth up to 4 health on top of its heal, but only if you are hurt when your turns start.

5 Combos and chains

Combos

A combo is a bonus when the chain runs from one of your pieces straight into another of yours along a link. The bonus lands on the second piece.

NameFromIntoBonus on the second piece

BaitTrickGuardplus the Trick's number

The rule that decides most turns

The chain starts at the piece you place and runs outward. So place the first piece of the pair next to the second, which is already on the board. To get Ambush, place the Trick next to a Hurt. To get Riposte, place the Guard next to a Hurt. Placing the Hurt next to the Trick gives no combo at all.

A piece reached through the Blank or through an environment piece has no combo partner. The creature gets the same combos from its own pieces.

How a chain fires

  1. Build the chain. Start at the piece just placed. Add every piece linked to it, newest first, and from each of those follow its own links. A piece is in a chain once.
  2. Fire in rank order (new in 2.7.00). Inside one rank, the build order holds.
1st2nd3rd4th5th
Environment piecesGuard, SootheTrickHurt, CharmHeal, Item
  • Combos still follow the build order: a piece's partner is the piece the chain came from, not the piece that fired just before it.
  • Each piece fires for its owner: yours for you, the creature's for the creature.
  • An environment piece fires for the side that started the chain.
  • If the fight ends in the middle of a chain, the rest does not fire.
  • The same rules hold for the creature's chain.
Figure 4. Rank order and a chain of 3.

The chain of 3 rule (use-up)

  • A chain that fires 3 or more pieces uses up every piece that fired. They leave the board at once.
  • It does not matter who started the chain or who owns the pieces. Environment pieces count and are used up too.
  • A chain of 1 or 2 uses nothing up. Those pieces stay and can fire again.

The other side's pieces in your chain

  • A creature Hurt in your chain hits you. A creature Guard gives it guard against your later Hurts. A creature Heal empties the bar. Its tags work for it.
  • But it also spends one of that piece's two firings. Firing a harmless creature piece is a cheap way to remove it.
  • Bait: the creature's aim is fixed before you move. Put a piece of yours with a fitting edge next to the aim cell, and the creature's chain will fire it, for you, with your combos. The aim chip shows it ("fires your Pierce"). It fails if the creature has Evade up: your Hurt misses.

6 The opening

Figure 5. The board at the start.
  1. The Blank sits on a random one of the four middle cells.
  2. You get your first hand of 5. Pick one piece and one side of the Blank.
  3. The creature puts its gentlest piece that is not a Hurt on a random side. If you chose the same side, yours stays and its piece moves to the next free side. A boss puts out two pieces.
  4. Nothing fires. Turn 1 begins with your other 4 pieces in hand.

Start with a Hurt

Put a Hurt out. On turn 1 you can then place a Trick or a Guard next to it for a combo. Before you confirm, find that partner in your hand and leave its cell free. The aim mark shown during the start pick is a placeholder: read the real aim once both start pieces are down.

7 The creature's turn

In order

  1. Its old guard and Evade drop (unless a Hold piece keeps the guard).
  2. Your poison on it ticks. If that fills the bar, you win before it acts.
  3. It plays its aim: the piece and the cell it showed you. If it cannot, it picks its best move now. If it is slowed (Trip, Lull), it plays nothing.
  4. Its chain fires, by the same rules as yours.
  5. Extras: one more piece after a Quick piece, a Water creature's push, a Plant's Bramble.
  6. The environment places one piece.
  7. Poison on you ticks, unless your Evade is still up. Then Simmer heals you. A trip takes effect.
  8. Old pieces leave (3 rounds). Venom cells fade.
  9. You draw a new hand (Keepers stay; 4 if tripped, 6 after Plenty). Your guard fades, except what Hold keeps.
  10. The new aim is shown. The creature gets no free piece in new fights.

The aim

  • The red mark is the cell. The chip names the piece and what its whole turn would do right now: "Peck: hits you for 9". It includes combos, Sun, Swarm, your guard, your pieces it fires and a Quick piece's second piece.
  • It does not include poison ticks on you or a Reptile's venom.
  • "· fires Sun" or "· fires Bramble": its chain would fire that environment piece. A ring marks it on the board.
  • The aim is a promise: the piece lands on that cell even if the cell now touches nothing.
You do thisThe creature does

9 The environment

  • After the creature's turn the environment places one piece. Its next piece is shown in the NEXT strip: you know what is coming, not where.
  • It goes on an empty cell with nothing on any side, among the three such cells farthest from all pieces. If none exists it takes a cell where it fits. With 12 or more cells taken it places nothing.
  • Its pieces belong to nobody. Link one into your chain and it fires for you. The creature can do the same.
  • They count for chain length and are used up by a chain of 3. Otherwise they stay 3 of your turns.

Which route?

Play Charm when the creature is gentle, when its guard is high, or when it dodges your next hit. Above small size a charm win is rare: Bramble, Thornburst and poison all count as hurt. Do not plan one unless your deck is built for it. Against a boss, Charm still fills the bar at x0.6 and ignores its guard.

Health carries over

  • You start with your expedition's health and leave with what is left. A fight won at 20% health makes the next fight on the trip much harder.
  • Heal and Item pieces, Mend, Simmer, Leech and a Rest outside the fight bring it back.
  • After the fight a result card shows. "Collect the reward" ends it. Tool wear is saved however the fight ended.

11 Building your deck

  • Your deck is built in the Puzzle box. It has three tabs: Kit, Belt, Pals. The deck is locked once the fight starts.
  • The first time, you pack a starter: 4 fixed pieces (Swing, Dodge, Rest, Charm) plus 6 of 12 offered pieces. Or tap a ready kit:
KitThe 6 pieces
Bold (default)your calling piece, Jab, a second Swing, Pierce, Snack, Oat Cake

  • Later you switch on more pieces you own: knacks, crop pieces, legendary crop pieces, craft pieces, region pieces. Filter buttons: All, basic, crop, a crown for legendary pieces, craft, region, wearables.

  • The deck bar shows "Hurt pieces: N of M" and warns under 3 in 10: "Few Hurt pieces: fights will be long".

Belt and pals

  • Each tool on your fight belt adds its one piece (axe: Chop; tilling tool: Hoe Hook; staff: Sweep; lantern: Glare...).
  • Belt slots: 2 for every gnome, 3 with the Stitched Fight Belt (level 8), 4 with the Masterwork Fight Belt (level 18).
  • A tool has 20 durability plus 4 per rank. Each play of its piece costs 2. A broken tool gives no piece: fix it with a Tool Repair Kit.
  • Each pal in your party lends one piece (for example Bark Badger, a Guard).

Legendary crop pieces

  • There are 42, one for each treasure crop. Harvest that crop 3 times and the piece is yours for good.
  • Its rank follows your food mastery of that crop, Common to Masterwork. Rare and Masterwork count toward the deck caps.
  • It needs a Larder slot. Its top and bottom edges are mind, like a crop piece.
  • They carry the only Keeper, Plenty and Simmer tags, and some carry a knack.
  • Legendary Heal pieces have mind edges on three sides: they only go beside mind knobs (always beside the Blank in round 1).
  • The three legendary Item pieces are not Snacks: they eat nothing and are never refused.

Cuts and ranks

  • Each cut is its own piece. Two copies of the same cut are one piece; the two cuts of one wearable are two pieces.
  • Ranks: Common, Lined (+2), Fine (+4). If you own two ranks of a cut, the best one is the piece.
  • A Fine piece counts as a Rare piece (the deck holds at most 3). You may choose to wear a Fine piece as Lined: then it is not Rare, and a little weaker.
  • Changing rank does not change the piece's on/off switch.

The Tailor

  • The Tailor is a garden building (garden level 6, 1800 Petals). You need one in at least one garden to craft Flood Boots, Work Gloves and a Travel Cloak, to line a wearable, and to re-cut one.
  • Lining (Common to Lined, then Lined to Fine) is a chance: 30%, plus 8% for each Tailor power point, up to 78%. Power is the sum of the levels of all your Tailors (each counts at most 3). Only the first 6 points help; points above 6 earn you commission Petals instead.
  • A failed lining gives a Tailor's Thread. 5 Threads make the next lining a sure thing.
  • Re-cutting to the other cut costs 1 Thread (free with a level 2 Tailor).
  • A level 3 Tailor makes your first wearable craft of the day for free.

13 Tips

  1. Can I win now? If a cell ends the fight, take it. Nothing else matters.
  2. Read the cost on the lit cell. Your chain can reach a creature Hurt (it hits you) or a Thornburst. The biggest number is often a trap. Poison is not in the aim number: against Reptiles, finish fast.
  3. Free guard first. A Guard placed next to a Hurt both blocks and attacks (Riposte). Firing a Rain gives guard on top of your move.
  4. Mind its guard and Evade. With a big "its guard" up, play Pierce or Charm. With "dodges next hit" up, lead with a weak Hurt or play another type.
  5. Take the chain of 3 when it adds clearly more, uses pieces in their last round, removes a dangerous creature piece, uses up a Sun or Rain before the creature fires it, or ends the fight. Keep a Bramble chain at 2 so it stays.
  6. Do not block for its own sake. Taking the aim cell makes it pick again at full strength. Block only big aims (Sun, combo, Bramble).
  7. Use bait. A fitting piece next to the aim cell is fired for you in its turn.
  8. Trip and Lull cost the creature a whole turn, and your poison still ticks.

14 Cheat sheet

Goal

  • Fill the Yield bar. Hurt and Charm both fill it. Its heal empties it (hurt first).
  • Keep your health above 0. It carries over to the next fight.

A round

  • You: one piece (or pass). Flip is free.
  • Creature: its aim, its chain, class extras.
  • Environment: one piece on a lonely cell.
  • Clean-up: poison, Simmer, old pieces leave, new hand of 5.

Fitting

  • Knob into hole, same family (round, keyhole, triangle).
  • Every touching edge must fit. A fit is a link.
  • Left is a hole, right is a knob. Flip swaps top and bottom.

Chains

  • The chain is the placed piece and everything linked to it.
  • It fires in rank order: environment, Guard/Soothe, Trick, Hurt/Charm, Heal/Item.
  • Each piece fires for its owner. Environment pieces fire for the starter.
  • 3 or more fired: all of them leave the board.
  • Pieces stay 3 rounds. Creature pieces fall after 2 firings.

Combos (place the first next to the second)

  • Trick into Hurt: x1.5 + Trick (Ambush).
  • Guard into Hurt: x1.5 (Riposte).
  • Hurt into Hurt: +2 (Double Swing).
  • Trick into Guard: + Trick (Bait).
  • Soothe into Charm: x1.5. Charm into Item: half as charm.

Before you place

  1. Can a cell end the fight?
  2. Aim chip: how much, where, "fires ..."? Poison on top?
  3. Its chips: guard, dodges next hit, thorny, Grudge, Swarm count.
  4. Lit cell: what does it cost me? (creature Hurt, Thornburst, venom)
  5. Flipped: is there a better cell?
  6. Sun, Rain, Bramble: who fires them first?
  7. After placing: what does the aim chip say now?

The aim

  • It lands on its cell unless your piece is on the cell, a misfit stands beside it, or it is slowed.
  • Then it picks again at full strength.
  • The number is its whole turn, without poison and venom.
  • Your fitting piece beside the aim cell is fired for you (bait).
  • No free piece for the creature in new fights.

Environment

  • Sun: the firing side's pieces count x2.
  • Bramble: the other side loses a tenth. Chain of 2 keeps it.
  • Rain: guard for the firing side (15%). Do not leave it to it.
  • Thornburst: hurts whoever fires it. Stay off it.

Classes

  • Critter: Hurt +1 per 2 other pieces (max +2).
  • Bird: two aims. Guard instead of chasing.
  • Beast: blocking makes it hit harder. Let small hits land.
  • Water: pushes your piece away.
  • Plant: grows a Bramble. Fire it first.
  • Reptile: venom cells and poison. Finish fast.
  • Boss: shows next round's piece. No charm win, no running, 24 turns.

Tags to remember

  • Keeper: stays in hand. Plenty: hand of 6 next round. Simmer: heal 2, twice.
  • Pierce: through guard. Trip, Lull: it skips a turn.
  • Evade: next Hurt misses; unused, it stops your poison tick.
  • Poison: half the printed number, 2 turns, flat.

Deck

  • At least 8 kit pieces and 1 Hurt. At most 3 Rare, 1 Masterwork.
  • Larder (crop): 3, 4 at level 10, 5 at 20.
  • Keepsakes (wearables): 1, 2 at 15, 3 at 25, 4 at 40.
  • New pieces arrive off, marked NEW.

Numbers

  • Board 4 by 4. Hand 5. One action. Fight limit 24 turns.
  • Charm x0.6 on wary and bosses. Hurt x0.8 on gentle.
  • Grudge +25% a block, up to 3. Venom 2 a round for 2 rounds.
  • Poison on you: at most 3 ticks wait.
