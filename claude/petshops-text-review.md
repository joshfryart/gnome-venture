## The 5 lines I am least sure about

1. **Abuela Quilla, SA**, 'Hola, mi cielo!' and 'Qué lindo': Spanish warmth is right for much of the region, but Brazil speaks Portuguese and highland communities speak Quechua/Aymara. Maybe add a neutral variant.
2. **Auntie Saffira, MENA**, 'habibi' / 'Mashallah' / 'Ma'a salama': widely used, but religious phrasing and one dialect cover a very diverse region. Consider dropping 'Mashallah'.
3. **Captain Pipi Shellback, OC**, 'G'day, little mate' / 'Beaut' / 'no dramas': Australian slang. It may read as one country standing for all of Australia, NZ and the Pacific. A Pacific or Maori shop voice could be better.
4. **Auntie Mossbead, WCA**, 'Akwaaba': Twi (Ghana). It is lovely, but WCA covers hundreds of languages, so it could read as one culture standing in for many. Possibly swap for a plainer welcome.
5. **Old Baobab Kito, ESA**, 'Karibu' / 'Slowly slowly': Swahili is East African; Southern Africa is the other half of the region. 'Slowly slowly' risks an 'unhurried Africa' cliche.

Also worth a glance: Babka Wren's gruffness (EE) and Nonna Briscola's 'You are too thin' (SE) lean on grandmother stereotypes, kept gentle on purpose.
